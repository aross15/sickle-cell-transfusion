# sickle-cell-transfusion
Sickle Cell pre-transfusion testing policies

Intended to be mostly the same as this OSF project: https://osf.io/9p46d/overview

Also see this file that unpacks SCD_survival.zip and processes the data, giving various csv files which are also uploaded here: https://colab.research.google.com/drive/1aqr9lQIEx18PB6OpdhzFkMHMcNn6bCQ6?usp=sharing

The article

Boshen Jiao, Kate M. Johnson, Scott D. Ramsey, M. A. Bender, Beth Devine, Anirban Basu; Long-term survival with sickle cell disease: a nationwide cohort study of Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries. Blood Adv 2023; 7 (13): 3276–3283. doi: https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2022009202

https://ashpublications.org/bloodadvances/article/7/13/3276/494890/Long-term-survival-with-sickle-cell-disease-a

contains graphs of survival curves for SCD patients in the USA. One of the authors, Anirban Basu , emailed me SCD_survival.zip that contains the data for the graphs. The email was on 2026-10-20.

Note that this is a “period” life table, rather than a “cohort” life table. The article says: “We used the Kaplan-Meier approach to estimate period life tables and lifetime survival curves for individuals with SCD.” For information on the difference between a period life table and cohort life table, see for example:

https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/lifeexpectancies/methodologies/periodandcohortlifeexpectancyexplained
https://ourworldindata.org/period-versus-cohort-measures-whats-the-difference
https://www.actuaries.asn.au/research-analysis/period-life-expectancy-vs-cohort-life-expectancy-the-difference-is-important

The article also says: "We included 94 616 individuals with SCD between 2008 and 2016, of whom 5% had Medicare OASI, 4% had Medicare DIB or ESRD, 48% had Medicaid, and 43% were dually eligible for Medicare and Medicaid (Table 1). "

It also says: “Most of the included individuals were Black (74%) and most resided in the South (53%) at the index date.”

It also says: “Further, we restricted the follow-up of our cohort to those receiving “common care,” consisting of all-comers who either are receiving no treatment or hydroxyurea or transfusions (but did not receive hematopoietic stem cell transplant)”
